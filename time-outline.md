# What Is Time? — Outline

*Working plan for the eighth exploration, October 2026. Arc settled before stage 1; open to steering.*

---

## Working claim to be broken

**Time is the parameter of change: one external order, the same for everything, in which the world evolves.**

Every exploration so far has leaned on this claim. Liouville's flow runs "in t." Von Neumann's chain unfolds "in t." A Turing machine takes its steps one after another. Space ended by grounding parts in a clock, and that clock is still assumed to tick against something.

The genealogy breaks the claim in three moves:
1. **Time stops being one.** Simultaneity becomes relative, and each worldline carries its own proper time.
2. **Time stops being external.** In canonical gravity the Hamiltonian is a constraint (HΨ = 0), so the state of the universe does not evolve. Time has to be recovered as a correlation between a clock subsystem and the rest.
3. **The clock stops being free.** No time operator exists, because energy is bounded below (Pauli). A clock is a physical subsystem with costs and limits: its ticks spread, and keeping time produces entropy.

## The question held in view: can the observer–world cut be derived?

Space reduced every division to one cut between a reference system and the rest. That cut was added by hand. The minimal reference system is a clock, so the question becomes what makes a subsystem a clock, and whether the dynamics selects one.

**Opening bet, to be tested, not a result:** the clock–world cut cannot be derived from H and ψ alone. A clock is a subsystem whose ticks are irreversible, redundant records, so **the arrow of time and the observer cut are one assumption, not two**. If that's right, the Past Hypothesis (entropy), redundancy as objectivity (measurement), and the observer's clock (space) are three views of a single input.

## 1. The intuition — flow, number, measure (full prehistory)

- Parmenides against Heraclitus; Zeno's arrow. Plato's *Timaeus*: time as the moving image of eternity, made with the heavens.
- Aristotle, *Physics* IV: time as the number of motion with respect to before and after, and the question of whether there would be time without a soul to count.
- Augustine, *Confessions* XI: he knows what time is until asked; past and future exist only as memory and expectation; time as *distentio animi*.
- Cyclic against linear time; the medieval debate over the eternity of the world.
- The mechanical clock (late 13th century, the escapement): time as something a machine divides into equal parts.
- Oresme's graphs and Galileo's falling bodies: t becomes a geometric axis. Huygens's pendulum clock (1656) and the isochronism problem.
- Harrison's chronometer and longitude: time as a transported convention (link to measurement's transport, not repeated).
- Newton's scholium: absolute, true, and mathematical time, flowing equably without relation to anything external. Leibniz: time as the order of succession. Kant: time as the form of inner sense. Cite Leibniz–Clarke and Kant, don't repeat them.
- What the intuition contains: one universal flow, independent of the clocks that measure it. That is the working claim.

## 2. The dispute — what does it mean for two clocks to agree?

- Practical pressure first: railway time, telegraph signals, standard time zones (1883), the Meridian Conference (1884). Synchronizing distant clocks becomes an engineering problem before it is a physics problem.
- Mach's attack on absolute time.
- Lorentz's "local time" (1895) as a mathematical aid; Poincaré's "La mesure du temps" (1898): simultaneity of distant events is a convention, chosen for convenience; Poincaré (1900) reads local time as what light-signal-synchronized clocks show.
- Einstein (1905): the synchronization procedure becomes the definition, and simultaneity becomes relative to a state of motion.
- Galison's thesis (*Einstein's Clocks, Poincaré's Maps*) that the patent office and the coordination of clocks shaped this. Influential and disputed; mark it as disputed.
- The afterlife: Reichenbach's ε and conventionality of simultaneity against Malament (1977), who argued that simultaneity is fixed by causal structure. Take a stance.
- Missing concepts: an operational definition of time, and an invariant that survives the loss of simultaneity (the interval).

## 3. The formalization — proper time as length

- Minkowski (1908): space and time merge; proper time is the Lorentzian length of a worldline, and the straight path is the longest. Worked example: the twin paradox at 0.8c (10 years against 6).
- General relativity: clocks run at rates set by the metric. Pound–Rebka (1959), Hafele–Keating (1971). Worked example: GPS, −7 μs/day from speed, +45 μs/day from altitude, net +38.
- Gödel's rotating universe (1949): closed timelike curves, and Gödel's argument that time is not objective. Link to *mathematical-platonism.md*.
- What it cost: no global time in a general spacetime. In the Hamiltonian form (Dirac; Arnowitt–Deser–Misner 1959–62), general covariance turns the Hamiltonian into a constraint. Time evolution becomes a gauge transformation. This is the hole argument (space §3) seen in time.

## 4. The abstraction — time as a relation (even weighting)

Three strands, given equal weight:

- **Canonical gravity.** Wheeler–DeWitt (1967) and the frozen formalism. Kuchař (1992) and Isham (1993) catalogue the problem of time. Relational observables (Rovelli's partial observables; Dittrich): what physics predicts is the value of one quantity when another reads a given value. Barbour's timelessness, mentioned briefly.
- **Quantum clocks.** Pauli's theorem: no self-adjoint time operator conjugate to a Hamiltonian bounded below. Page–Wootters (1983): a static global state, with evolution appearing in the correlation between a clock and the rest. Worked example: a two-state clock and a qubit, conditioning on the clock reading. Kuchař's objections, the Giovannetti–Lloyd–Maccone repair (2015), and the Höhn–Smith–Lock "trinity" (2021) showing three formulations of relational dynamics to be equivalent.
- **Thermal time.** Connes–Rovelli (1994): Tomita–Takesaki modular flow assigns a time flow to any state on a von Neumann algebra. *The state defines the time.* Worked example: for a Gibbs state, the modular flow is ordinary time evolution rescaled by temperature. Direct link to space §4 (type III algebras), and to entropy (the state that defines the time is a thermal one).

The question for this stage: are these three the same answer seen from three sides, or competing answers?

## 5. The payoff — the clock as observer

*Before writing this stage: search for 2025–26 developments on each thread below, so the payoff isn't built only on prior knowledge.*

- Recap from space (no second pass): the crossed product and the observer's clock in de Sitter (Chandrasekaran–Longo–Penington–Witten 2022). Here the question is what the clock's Hamiltonian being bounded below does: Pauli's theorem turned from an obstacle into a structural ingredient.
- Quantum reference frames (Giacomini–Castro-Ruiz–Brukner 2019) and the observer-dependence of gravitational entropy (De Vuyst–Eccles–Höhn–Kirklin 2024).
- The thermodynamics of timekeeping: autonomous quantum clocks (Erker et al. 2017) and the measured entropy cost per tick (Pearson et al. 2021). The direct test of the bet: does a good clock have to produce entropy?
- Clocks in superposition: time dilation inside an interferometer (Zych et al. 2011); gravitationally entangled clocks (Castro-Ruiz–Giacomini–Brukner 2017); optical clocks resolving redshift across a millimetre.
- Indefinite causal order (Oreshkov–Costa–Brukner 2012; the quantum switch): when the order of events itself is in superposition.
- Search targets for 2025–26: observer algebras and clocks in de Sitter; quantum reference frames; the thermodynamic cost of timekeeping; indefinite causal order experiments; clock tests of relativity; the problem of time.
- The endpoint question: is time a parameter, a relation, or what a record-keeping subsystem is?
