# What Is Measurement?

*Notes from a conversation, September 2026. From the royal cubit to Weyl's rod to Wigner's friend to the horizon.*

---

## 1. A working definition

**A measurement is the creation of a record: a stable correlation between a system and something else, which can be compared with other records. A result is objective to the degree its record is redundant, and comparable only within limits set by causal structure and computation.**

The first clause is Szilard and Bennett, the second Zurek, the third Harlow–Hayden. The intuition had none of them. It had *comparison with a standard*, a practice so successful that for most of history nobody thought it needed a theory.

Working claim broken by the genealogy: **measurement is comparison under a transported convention.** It fails three times, in three different ways — repaired, reconstructed, and not repaired (§6).

Distinctions worth keeping:

- **Measuring vs. counting.** Greek *megethos* vs. *arithmos*. A magnitude need not be a number of units (the diagonal); Eudoxus handled it by comparison alone.
- **Standard vs. convention.** An artefact standard (the metre bar, the kilogram) must be transported; a law-based standard (the caesium frequency) is reproduced anywhere. The 2019 SI is the switch from one to the other.
- **Disturbance vs. definition.** Heisenberg's microscope: the value exists and the measurement kicks it. Bohr: the value is defined only relative to an arrangement. Kochen–Specker sides with Bohr.
- **Proper vs. improper mixture.** Same density matrix; one is ignorance of a fact, the other is a part of an entangled whole with no fact to be ignorant of. The measurement problem is the question which one you have.
- **Decoherence vs. outcome.** Decoherence explains why you can't see the superposition and in which basis things look definite. It doesn't explain why there is one result.
- **Objectivity vs. absoluteness.** Objectivity: many observers agree (redundancy). Absoluteness: the result is a fact for everyone, not relative to anything. Local Friendliness shows the second can fail where the first was never established.

Pattern: unlike entropy (formula first) and randomness (metaphysics first, mathematics alongside), measurement began as a working practice with no theory, acquired a classical theory only in 1901 (Hölder), and had it overturned within thirty years. (Position.)

## 2. The intuition — comparison with a standard

### Three strands that didn't know they were one

**The standard.** Egyptian cubit rods checked against a master cubit: measurement is comparison, authority lives in the standard. The Revolution tried to move authority from king to nature. The metre of 1791 was one ten-millionth of the meridian quadrant, surveyed Dunkirk–Barcelona by Delambre and Méchain, 1792–98 (Méchain hid a discrepancy in his own latitudes for life). Cast as the *mètre des Archives* (1799), and from then on the bar, not the Earth, was the metre. Metre Convention (1875); platinum-iridium prototypes (1889). Maxwell (1870): permanent standards should be sought in the wavelength, period, and mass of molecules, identical everywhere. The program took until 2019.

**Measuring vs. counting.** The diagonal has no common unit with the side, so a magnitude need not be a number. Eudoxus (Euclid Book V): equality of ratios defined by comparing all integer multiples, with the Archimedean axiom. Two thousand years of a theory of continuous measurement that avoided saying what the result was.

**What comparison can detect.** Leibniz: similar figures can't be told apart considered separately; size difference appears only in *compresence*, when perceived together. Leibniz–Clarke: magnitude and position are relations. Poincaré: double everything overnight and nothing registers it; only ratios are observable. Kant (1768): left and right hands agree on every internal measurement and can't be superposed — a real difference invisible to magnitude. Lesson: handedness is a convention that can't be transmitted by description (Gardner's Ozma problem).

### Helmholtz and Hölder

Helmholtz, "Counting and Measuring" (1887): arithmetic applies to experience when there is a physical combination (rods end to end, weights in a pan) that behaves like addition. Hölder (1901): an ordered, Archimedean structure with such a combination is isomorphic to a substructure of the positive reals, unique up to choice of unit. Eudoxus's Book V completed as a representation theorem, and the first precise definition: **measurement is a structure-preserving map from an empirical comparison structure into the reals, fixed up to a unit.**

A small dispute: Fechner (1860) claimed to measure sensation; the Ferguson Committee (1932–40) split because sensations have no concatenation; Stevens (1946) redefined measurement as assignment of numbers by rule, with scale types (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) classified by admissible transformations — Klein's Erlangen program applied to scales. Endpoint: the representational theory (Krantz, Luce, Suppes, Tversky, 1971), the complete classical formalization, which never meets quantum mechanics.

### Weyl's rod (1918)

General relativity compares directions at a distance only by transport along a path (path-dependence is curvature) but still compared lengths with no path. Weyl: let the length unit vary from point to point; a rod carried around a loop may return a different length; the transport rule's mathematics matched the electromagnetic potential. The first gauge theory.

Einstein's objection: if a rod's length depended on its history, so would an atom's clock rate, and spectral lines would be smeared. They are sharp. **Atoms are standards whose readings don't depend on their history.** Weyl's scale gauge failed; its mathematics survived with quantum phase (London, Weyl). Here it matters as the point where "transport doesn't matter" was shown to be a physical assumption — true for lengths because atoms make it true.

### The 2019 redefinition

Metre via krypton-86 (1960); second via caesium (1967); metre via fixed c (1983). On 20 May 2019: Δν_Cs, c, h, e, k, N_A, K_cd fixed exactly; every unit defined through them. The International Prototype Kilogram retired — it had drifted about 50 μg against its copies over a century, but was by definition exactly one kilogram. An artefact standard can't be wrong about itself.

- *Measuring a constant* changed meaning: in 2018 h was measured; since 2019 it is exact and the kilogram is measured. A redefinition of equality hidden inside a precision upgrade.
- k is an exact conversion factor between temperature and energy; Landauer's kT ln 2 per bit is an exact number of joules.

*Position:* the Duff–Okun–Veneziano debate (2002; zero, two, or three fundamental dimensionful constants) goes to Duff. Fixing h, c, k is a choice of units — a gauge choice — and the only measured content of physics is dimensionless ratios. Leibniz and Poincaré operationalized. **Standards stopped being transported when a law made transport unnecessary.** Kant's hands resolve the same way: Wu's cobalt-60 experiment (1957) solves the Ozma problem because the weak interaction breaks parity. A convention becomes measurable when nature supplies the standard — by atomic identity for length, by symmetry breaking for handedness.

### What was assumed without saying

1. **Comparison does not disturb.** The instrument is outside the physics.
2. **The value exists before the comparison.** Hölder maps magnitudes that are already there — the Boolean assumption of the randomness notes.
3. **Any two things can be brought together.** Leibniz's compresence assumes one observer who can perceive any pair; the SI assumes any laboratory can realize any standard.

The missing concept: **the apparatus as a physical system.** Until the ruler obeys the same laws as what it measures, measurement is something done *to* physics, not something that happens *within* it.

## 3. The dispute — where does the measurement happen?

### Background: Bohr's cut

Heisenberg's microscope (1927): the value exists, the photon kicks it. Bohr (Como, 1927): position and momentum are defined only relative to mutually exclusive arrangements — complementarity. And: apparatus and results must be described classically, because that is the only language in which a result can be communicated unambiguously. Heisenberg's *Schnitt*, the cut. Bohr could win the Solvay debates (1927, 1930 photon box) and answer EPR (1935) only because he never said what the apparatus was made of.

### The chain (von Neumann, 1932)

The apparatus is a quantum system obeying Process 2 (unitary). A good measurement copies: |sᵢ⟩|m₀⟩ → |sᵢ⟩|mᵢ⟩. By linearity a superposition gives Σ cᵢ|sᵢ⟩|mᵢ⟩: the apparatus is entangled, not pointing. Add light, retina, nerve, cortex; each link unitary; the superposition spreads. Process 2 never produces an outcome.

Von Neumann's answer was a theorem: split into I (system), II (apparatus), III (observer); applying Process 1 at I | II+III or at I+II | III gives identical predictions, up to the "abstract ego." The cut must be somewhere; its location is empirically irrelevant.

**Why the proof works, and what it hides.** Tracing out the apparatus gives ρ_S = Σ |cᵢ|² |sᵢ⟩⟨sᵢ| — exactly the post-collapse mixture. The cut is movable because entanglement already makes S look collapsed. But this is an **improper** mixture (d'Espagnat): the whole is in one pure state and there is no fact which |sᵢ⟩ the part is in. The theorem identifies two situations no local measurement distinguishes — the silent casualty of equality again, as L² identified functions differing on measure zero.

### Pushing the cut upward

- **London–Bauer (1939):** consciousness closes the chain; the observer knows their own state by introspection.
- **Schrödinger (1935):** the cat, from Einstein's gunpowder, as a reductio; the same paper coins *Verschränkung*.
- **Wigner (1961):** the friend in the sealed lab sees a result; Wigner outside assigns an entangled state; both can't be right unless consciousness collapses. Wigner dropped this in the 1970s after decoherence.
- **Deutsch (1985):** the friend is a reversible computer that records "I saw a definite outcome" without recording which; Wigner undoes the measurement and tests for interference. Collapse-at-the-friend and no-collapse predict different results. The cut gets an empirical address, for an observer who can reverse another.

### The downward answer

Daneri, Loinger, Prosperi (1962), after Ludwig: an apparatus is macroscopic; registering a result is irreversible thermodynamic amplification; interference is suppressed by recurrence-scale factors. Wigner (1963): practically zero is not zero. Bell, "Against 'Measurement'" (1990): **FAPP** — every such account shows the superposition can't be detected and treats undetectable as nonexistent. It answers why you can't see the superposition, not why there is one outcome.

*Position:* Bell is right about the logic, and DLP's irreversibility is coarse-graining. The measurement problem and the problem of the second law's direction have the same structure: reversible dynamics (Liouville, unitarity) preserves everything; a partial description sees something one-way (entropy increase, an outcome). Von Neumann posed both: the quantum H-theorem (1929) required choosing macroscopic observables; the movable cut (1932) is the same unassigned choice.

### Why it couldn't be resolved

Three missing concepts:

1. **What a record is** — a correlation that is stable, copyable, and readable by several observers without disturbance. Bohr's communicability, DLP's irreversibility, Wigner's report all point at it; nobody defined it.
2. **Proper vs. improper mixture** — without it the consistency theorem looks like a solution; with it, the theorem restates the problem.
3. **The observer as a physical system** — London–Bauer and Wigner gave consciousness a special role; Deutsch's reversible friend makes the question physics.

*Position on Bohr:* right about what was missing and wrong to make it a primitive. The classical-language requirement is a claim about records, made immune to analysis by being stated as an epistemological necessity.

*Position on von Neumann:* the theorem did more harm than good for thirty years — by proving the cut's location empirically irrelevant it made the question look metaphysical, as his flawed no-hidden-variables proof made Bohm look impossible.

The dispute ends not with a winner but with a change of subject: from where the cut is to what physically happens to the entangled state.

## 4. The formalization — what the environment knows

### The operational layer

POVMs: outcomes correspond to positive operators Eᵢ summing to the identity, P(i) = Tr(ρEᵢ); projective measurements a special case. Kraus operators give the post-measurement state; an *instrument* (Davies–Lewis, 1970) combines both. **Naimark's dilation theorem**: every POVM is a projective measurement on a larger system; Stinespring does the same for state changes. Every generalized measurement is a von Neumann chain truncated at a convenient point. The framework catalogues measurements and says nothing about where the chain stops.

### Decoherence

**Zeh (1970):** macroscopic objects are never isolated; every scattered molecule or photon is a small von Neumann interaction. The environment extends the chain uninvited. **Joos–Zeh (1985):** a dust grain in air loses positional interference on timescales far below anything measurable; the microwave background alone suffices.

**Einselection (Zurek, 1981–82).** The chain has a hidden second problem: Σ cᵢ|sᵢ⟩|mᵢ⟩ can often be rewritten in other bases (infinitely many when coefficients are equal), so unitarity doesn't say which observable was measured — the *preferred-basis problem*. The system–environment interaction picks it: **pointer states** are those least entangled by monitoring, roughly eigenstates of what the environment couples to. Scattering couples to position, so pointer states are localized wave packets. The predictability sieve (Zurek, Habib, Paz, 1993): pointer states produce least entropy under monitoring. Decoherence yields the improper mixture of §3, in the right basis. It explains Bell's FAPP with a mechanism; it doesn't produce an outcome.

**Observed:** Brune et al. (Haroche, 1996), cavity field superposition decaying at a rate set by the separation of its components; Hackermüller et al. (2004), heated C₇₀ fullerenes losing interference as thermal photons carry which-path information away.

### Quantum Darwinism

Zurek (2003 review; Ollivier–Poulin–Zurek 2004; Blume-Kohout–Zurek 2006; *Nature Physics* 2009). **The environment is not a sink but the channel through which we observe everything.** Nobody measures a chair; everyone intercepts a different fraction of the photons that scattered off it, and all agree without disturbing it. What structure must the global state have for this to happen?

**The quantity.** Split the environment into fragments F; compute the quantum mutual information I(S:F) = H(S) + H(F) − H(S,F) as a function of fragment size. The shape of the curve is the theory.

**Toy model** (one system qubit in equal superposition, ten environment qubits, each rotated by θ if the system is |1⟩):

| Qubits in fragment | 0 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| θ = 90° (perfect copies) | 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 2.00 |
| θ = 45° (partial) | 0 | 0.60 | 0.81 | 1.00 | 1.40 | 2.00 |
| θ = 22.5° (weak) | 0 | 0.26 | 0.44 | 0.85 | 1.43 | 1.69 |

With perfect copies one qubit gives the full classical bit; ten disjoint fragments each hold it; the curve plateaus; only the whole environment gives 2 bits = 2H(S), the extra bit being the quantum phase, recoverable only globally. The improper mixture of §3 as geometry: locally classical and redundant, globally coherent. With weak copies there is barely a plateau and decoherence is incomplete. Objectivity comes in degrees.

**Redundancy** R_δ: the number of disjoint fragments each supplying all but a fraction δ of the classical information. Riedel and Zurek (2010): a micron-sized dust grain in sunlight has its position recorded on the order of a hundred million times within about a microsecond.

**Why only pointer states.** No-cloning: a unitary can make many copies only of a set of mutually orthogonal states — one basis. Superpositions can't be spread into many fragments. The fittest states, those leaving the most descendants, are pointer states, and only they become objective. Brandão, Piani, Horodecki (2015): for generic interactions, fragment-readers can learn only about one preferred observable and will agree on it. The Horodeckis characterized the required state: **spectrum broadcast structure**. Small-scale demonstrations of the plateau: photonic (Ciampini et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2019), NV centre (Unden et al., 2019) — environments of a handful of qubits.

### What it supplies

The first missing concept: **a record is information about a pointer observable held redundantly in many independently readable fragments.** Bohr's communicability becomes a count of copies; DLP's irreversibility becomes the practical impossibility of gathering every fragment.

It rebuilds the stage-2 classical picture as a regime: comparison doesn't disturb (observers read copies); the value exists before you look (for the pointer observable only — Kochen–Specker still holds); anyone can compare (who shares the environment).

*Position:* **Boolean event structure is what survives copying.** Kolmogorov's 𝓕 is not a basic feature of the world but the set of observables whose information can be redundantly broadcast.

### What it cost

1. *It solves objectivity, not outcomes.* Within a branch observers agree; across branches there is no fact. Redundancy makes the improper mixture more robust, not proper. Zurek's "existential interpretation" is close to Everett, accurately; quantum Darwinism is the best current account of what a branch is.
2. *The cut became a factorization.* Everything assumes a split into system, environment, and fragments; change the tensor-product structure and the plateaus vanish. The factorization problem (Carroll and Singh on quantum mereology, 2021). Division, a fourth time, and most basic: now needed to define parts.
3. *The Born rule is argued, not cleanly derived.* Envariance (2003–05) against Schlosshauer–Fine (2005). Valuable as Gleason is: it shows the rule must take this form, not why any outcome occurs.

"Darwinism" is vivid and loose: selection and copying, no variation or inheritance. "Quantum broadcasting" would be more accurate.

**Structural result: objectivity equals redundancy.**

## 5. The abstraction — measurement with the apparatus removed

With no physical mark of a measuring device, two opposite answers. Both drop collapse; both deny the cut is physical.

### Measurement as correlation — Everett

Prehistory: Szilard's two-state memory (1929), Bennett's measurement-as-copy (1982). Neither refers to an observer beyond a register and a correlation.

**Everett (1957):** the chain, uncut. The universal state evolves unitarily always; an observer is an automaton with memory; a measurement correlates memory and system. No absolute state of the system, only **relative states**. Within each term: repeated measurements agree; different observers agree (quantum Darwinism in embryo, fifty years early); the branching is imperceptible (Everett's Copernicus footnote). Wheeler cut the thesis to a quarter under pressure from Bohr's circle; Copenhagen visit (1959) cool; Everett left physics. DeWitt's "many worlds" (1970) is not Everett's term.

**Preferred basis:** answered by §4. Wallace, *The Emergent Multiverse* (2012): branches are emergent, approximately defined patterns, real as a traffic jam is. **The number of branches is not well-defined.**

**Probability** splits into the incoherence problem (how is there uncertainty if everything happens?) and the quantitative problem (why |c|²?). Branch counting isn't even a rule. *Decision theory* (Deutsch 1999, Wallace 2012): rational agents must weight branches by |c|² — Savage with branches, resting on branching indifference, disputed by Albert, Kent, Price. *Self-location* (Vaidman; Sebens–Carroll 2018): after branching and before looking, there are several of you and you don't know which you are; |c|² from the requirement that self-locating credences not depend on distant independent systems.

*Position:* the quantitative problem is solved about as well as Gleason solved the Born rule. The incoherence problem is answered by self-location, and the cost is that **an outcome becomes an indexical fact, like "here" or "now."**

Everett is the only view that completes Bell's wish: "measurement" is not a primitive, a process, or a type of event, but a coarse-grained label for interactions producing stable, redundantly recorded correlations with a memory.

### Measurement as an agent's act — QBism

Caves, Fuchs, Schack (2002), the quantum de Finetti theorem; Fuchs, Mermin, Schack (2014). Claims: a state belongs to an agent and encodes their expectations about consequences of their actions; a measurement is an action; an outcome is the experience returned to the agent who acted, personal; collapse is Bayesian updating; quantum theory is a normative addition to probability theory.

**The urgleichung.** Fix a reference SIC-POVM in dimension d (d² outcomes); your probabilities p(i) for it determine your state. For a second measurement with outcomes j, let r(j|i) be your probability of j given reference outcome i. Classical total probability: q(j) = Σ p(i) r(j|i). The Born rule:

q(j) = Σᵢ [(d+1) p(i) − 1/d] r(j|i), which for a projective measurement is (d+1) Σ p(i) r(j|i) − 1.

All of quantum theory's departure from classical probability in one dimension-dependent stretch and shift. Caveat: SIC existence in every dimension is Zauner's conjecture, open.

**Stage 4 reinterpreted.** Decoherence as van Fraassen's reflection principle (Fuchs–Schack 2012): expecting to lose track of the environment, assign now a state without interference. Agreement is intersubjective convergence among agents who interact, not copies in a state nobody possesses.

**Wigner's friend, dissolved:** the friend's outcome is the friend's experience; Wigner's superposition is Wigner's expectation; no view from nowhere, so nothing to disagree about.

**Objections:** solipsism (reply: the world is what resists the agent — "participatory realism," after Wheeler); explanatory thinness (why does the rule work, why this deformation? The reply that the deformation *is* the fact about the world is honest and places all of physics in one unexplained coherence rule).

### The contrast

| | Everett | QBism |
|---|---|---|
| Keeps | The universal state | The agent |
| Removes | The observer as special | The state as a property of the world |
| Measurement is | A correlation with a memory | An action with a personal consequence |
| An outcome is | An indexical fact (which branch) | An experience (what happened to you) |
| Collapse is | Nothing | Updating |
| Born rule | Rational betting, or self-location | Normative deformation of total probability |
| Decoherence, redundancy | Physical; define the branches | A reasoning rule; agents converge |
| Wigner's friend | Both correct; Wigner could interfere the branches | Both correct; no fact spans the agents |
| The cut | Nowhere | Wherever an agent acts |

Shared: **both give up the idea that an outcome is an absolute fact** — relative to a branch, or to an agent.

*Positions:*
- Everett is the stronger abstraction of measurement; QBism the stronger abstraction of probability. They answer different questions, which is why neither displaces the other.
- QBism repeats Bohr's move with experience in place of classical language: a primitive refused analysis. More coherent than Bohr, since any agent qualifies; structurally the same. The QBist reply — some primitive is unavoidable, and theirs is honest — deserves weight.
- Everett's cost is that personal identity forks; the ordinary sense of one future is a coarse-grained description, like a macrostate.
- *Tentative:* the Everett–QBism split is the entropy notes' "system or knowledge" dichotomy one level down — outside and inside views of one structure.

## 6. The payoff — what no single observer can compare

### Wigner's friend becomes a theorem

Two sealed labs, two friends sharing an entangled pair; outside each, a Wigner who either asks the friend or reverses the whole lab and measures in another basis. Brukner (2018): observer-independent facts can't coexist with quantum predictions, given locality and free choice. Frauchiger–Renner (2018): four agents each reasoning correctly with quantum mechanics reach contradictory conclusions.

**Local Friendliness (Bong et al., 2020):** from (1) *absoluteness of observed events*, (2) *no superdeterminism*, (3) *locality*, inequalities follow that quantum mechanics violates. Unlike Bell, **no assumption that outcomes are determined by prior variables** — escaping Bell by rejecting hidden variables doesn't escape this. A six-photon demonstration, with path qubits as "friends"; the real test needs a human-level AI friend on a reversible quantum computer (Wiseman, Cavalcanti, Rieffel, 2023), Deutsch's 1985 proposal grown up.

Who pays: Everett and QBism drop absoluteness; Bohm drops locality; superdeterminism drops free choice; collapse theories avoid the violation, since a real friend's measurement collapses and can't be reversed — which makes the AI-friend experiment a test of collapse. **The loss of absolute outcomes that Everett and QBism accept is forced.**

*Position:* **observed events are absolute to the degree that their records are redundant.** Wigner's reversal needs zero copies outside the lab; a single escaping photon destroys the effect. The sealed lab is an environment with zero outside redundancy; sunlight on a dust grain is the opposite limit. Like Boolean structure, absoluteness is an emergent property of widely copied events, not a basic feature of events.

*Tentative:* the theorem is compatible with both Everett and QBism, and constrains their reconciliation — Wigner's outside view becomes operational only by erasing the friend's inside view. If the two are views of one structure, they are complementary in Bohr's original sense.

### Horizons as measurement boundaries

**Complementarity (Susskind, Thorlacius, Uglum, 1993).** A message falls into a black hole: the infalling observer sees a smooth horizon and keeps it; the outside observer, if evaporation is unitary, sees it return scrambled in the radiation. Two copies violate no-cloning; no single observer sees both. Bohr's word, moved from arrangements to spacetime regions. Hayden–Preskill (2007): old black holes return information after only the scrambling time (logarithmic in entropy); for the outside observer to jump in and meet the interior copy, it would need to be sent with more than Planckian energy. Complementarity survived narrowly.

**Firewall (AMPS, 2012).** After the Page time, each new Hawking mode must be entangled with the early radiation (unitarity) and with its partner behind the horizon (smoothness). Monogamy forbids both: give up unitarity, smoothness, or physics outside the horizon. New element: a single observer could in principle check both — distil from outside, then jump in. Complementarity doesn't protect this.

**Harlow–Hayden (2013).** Distilling the needed piece of the early radiation is a quantum computation that for a generic black hole takes time exponential in its entropy, under standard complexity assumptions; Aaronson tied it to breaking post-quantum one-way functions. Evaporation takes polynomial time. The comparison is allowed by law, allowed by causal structure, and blocked by complexity. Whether this is the full resolution is debated.

*Position:* **what can be compared is bounded by what can be computed** — the newest idea in the exploration, and a third kind of limit on measurement after law and causal structure. Work since 2019 argues Hawking radiation is computationally pseudorandom: a pure state no efficient observer can distinguish from thermal noise. Yao's pseudorandomness at the horizon. The pattern across four explorations: entropy relative to macrovariables tracked; randomness relative to computational power; objectivity relative to redundancy; measurability relative to complexity.

### What the intuition could never have said

The working claim fails three times:

1. **Transport** failed with Weyl; atoms rescued it; the 2019 SI made it law. *Repaired.*
2. **Non-disturbing comparison with pre-existing values** failed with quantum mechanics; Zurek rebuilt it as the regime of redundant records; Local Friendliness marks where it ends. *Reconstructed.*
3. **That any two things can be compared** fails outright, twice: Wigner can't compare with the friend without erasing the friend's record; no one can compare radiation with interior before the black hole is gone. *Not repaired* — and complementarity, introduced as a philosophical stance, returns as a structural fact about records, spacetime, and computation.

What survives from stage 1 is **Leibniz's compresence**: some differences exist only for what can be perceived together. Physics now specifies what can be perceived together: records must be redundant to be absolute, in one causal region to be compared, and within computational reach before the time runs out.

*Closing position:* **a measurement result is a record, and physics is increasingly a theory of which records can be brought together, by whom, and at what cost.** "What is measurement?" turns into "what is the structure of comparability in the world?" — the division theme, reached directly.

## 7. The single line of thought

One question, asked with steadily less confidence that everything can be brought together: what does it take to compare two things, and who can do it?

| Stage | Measurement is… | What is compared | What it yields |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cubit / metre bar | comparison with an artefact | a thing with a transported standard | trade, surveying, authority by object |
| Eudoxus → Hölder (−370 to 1901) | a structure-preserving map to the reals | magnitudes, under concatenation | the classical theory, fixed up to a unit |
| Weyl / Einstein / SI (1918–2019) | reproduction of a law-based standard | dimensionless ratios only | standards that need no transport |
| Bohr / von Neumann (1927–32) | a cut in a unitary chain | the system, through an apparatus whose own state is left undescribed | the measurement problem; the movable cut |
| Zeh / Zurek (1970–2009) | redundant broadcast into the environment | copies of pointer states, read independently | records, objectivity, Boolean structure as emergent |
| Everett / QBism (1957–2014) | a correlation, or an agent's act | branches, or experiences | outcomes as indexical, or personal |
| Local Friendliness (2018–20) | — | records that can't coexist as absolute facts | absoluteness as emergent from redundancy |
| Horizons (1993–2013) | a comparison limited by causal structure and complexity | radiation and interior | complementarity as structure; measurement bounded by computation |

Waves kept the space and lost the substrate. Symmetry kept the group and lost the object. Entropy kept the logarithm and lost the engine, the gas, and the ignorance. Randomness kept the absence and lost the cause, the reason, the sequence, the sample space, and the definite outcome. Measurement kept the *record* and lost the standard, the apparatus, the cut, the absolute outcome, and finally the guarantee that any two records can ever be brought together.
