# What Is Measurement? — Outline

*Working plan for the fifth exploration, September 2026. Arc confirmed before stage 1.*

---

## Working claim to be broken

Classically, **measurement is comparison under a transported convention**: a quantity is measured by carrying a standard to it. The genealogy is that claim failing, first because the standard cannot be transported (Weyl), then because the comparison changes the thing compared (quantum mechanics), then because not every comparison can be carried out by any one observer (horizons).

---

## 1. The intuition — comparison with a standard

- Leibniz's indistinguishables; Kant's hands. An internal description can't fix handedness, so comparison needs a convention carried from elsewhere.
- Helmholtz, "Counting and Measuring" (1887); Hölder (1901). The first axioms for what makes a quantity measurable at all.
- Metrology, kept narrow:
  - Weyl's 1918 scale gauge: does a rod carried around a loop come back the same length?
  - Einstein's objection: atoms are path-independent standards.
  - The 2019 SI fixes h, e, k, c, and Δν_Cs, so Einstein's objection becomes law.
  - k becomes an exact energy–information conversion factor.
- Guardrail: if metrology grows its own momentum, it becomes a side file.

## 2. The dispute — where does the measurement happen?

- Bohr–Einstein, from Solvay 1927 to EPR (1935) and Bohr's reply; complementarity.
- Von Neumann's chain (1932) and the movable cut: the theory is indifferent to where the cut goes.
- Wigner's friend (1961): the regress made personal.
- Missing concepts to identify: what counts as a record; what counts as an observer; whether the cut is physical.

## 3. The formalization — what measurement is, mathematically

- The von Neumann measurement scheme: measurement as a unitary interaction followed by a projection.
- Generalized measurements (POVMs), quantum operations, and instruments (Davies–Lewis, Kraus).
- Decoherence (Zeh, Zurek): einselection and pointer states. It explains why outcomes look classical and in which basis, but not why a single outcome occurs.
- What it cost: to be determined in stage 3.

## 4. The abstraction — two ways to strip the picture

- **Measurement as correlation:** Szilard's memory, Bennett's measurement-as-copy, and Everett's relative states. The chain without the cut.
- **Measurement as an agent's act:** QBism (Caves–Fuchs–Schack), with the quantum de Finetti theorem, Gleason as Dutch book, and the Born rule as a coherence condition.
- This is the full Everett/QBism treatment deferred by *what-is-randomness.md*.

## 5. The payoff — limits on who can measure what

- **Extended Wigner's friend:** Frauchiger–Renner (2018); Local Friendliness (Bong et al., 2020), with its experimental tests. Bell-type theorems about observers rather than outcomes.
- **Horizons as measurement boundaries**, framed as measurement and not as entropy:
  - Black-hole complementarity (Susskind, 1993): no single observer sees both copies of the information.
  - AMPS firewall (2012).
  - Harlow–Hayden (2013): the decoding required outlasts the evaporation, so measurement is bounded by computational complexity.
